Go-to-market strategy

Most energy businesses chase too many markets at once. We help you pick the ones you can actually win, then build one plan to win them.

Start a conversation

A go-to-market plan should answer three questions in plain English: who are we selling to, why should they pick us, and how do we reach them. If your team would give you three different answers, that is the problem we fix.

Start with an honest look at the market

We look at where demand is real, where competitors are weak and where your strengths give you a genuine edge. Not where you would like to be, or where the last board paper said you should be.

From there we split the market into customer groups worth your time and the ones that are not. Saying no to a sector is usually the hardest part of the exercise, and the most valuable.

Then decide how to reach them

Once the target is clear, we work out the route. How the proposition is positioned for each buyer, which messages land with the people who sign, which channels actually produce meetings, and how long it takes to build a pipeline that holds.

This is built on what works in energy and complex B2B selling, where buying cycles are long, funding matters and the decision often sits with a finance director who never attends the first meeting.

One plan, not three

The output is a single plan that links proposition, sales and marketing. Your teams know where to focus, what to stop doing and how progress will be measured. It is short enough to be read, and specific enough to be used.

Common questions

Quick answers

What is go-to-market strategy?

A go-to-market strategy is the plan for how a business wins customers: which markets and customers to target, what to offer them, and how to reach and sell to them.

When does a business need it?

The usual signs: sales chase anything with a pulse and win rates are falling; marketing produce activity that sales do not use; the board keeps adding new markets without dropping old ones; nobody can say in one sentence who your ideal customer is.

What do you get at the end?

Typically: target market and segment priorities, with the ones to drop; positioning and key messages for each buyer group; channel and route-to-market plan; pipeline targets and the measures that track progress; a 90-day action plan with named owners. See how pricing works.

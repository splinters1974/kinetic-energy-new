Bid and pursuit strategy

Complex deals are rarely won on price. They are won on qualification, a clear win strategy and a proposal that answers what the customer actually scores.

Start a conversation

Too many teams respond to every tender that lands. The quickest way to raise your win rate is to stop bidding for work you were never going to win, and put that effort into the bids you can.

Qualify before you commit

We look at every opportunity through a hard bid or no-bid lens. Do we know the customer? Do we know why they are buying? Can we win on their terms, and will we make money if we do? Bids that fail the test get dropped early, before the team has spent three weeks on them.

Build a win strategy

For the opportunities that pass, we help shape how you win. What drives the decision, who you are up against, where your strengths line up with the customer's priorities and where you need to create separation. The aim is to lead the conversation, not just respond to the question.

Write proposals that score

We support proposal development with a focus on clarity, outcomes and commercial discipline. Bids that are easier to read, easier to score and harder for competitors to undercut. That includes public sector frameworks, where the scoring model decides everything.

Common questions

Quick answers

What is bid and pursuit strategy?

Bid and pursuit strategy is how a business decides which tenders and large deals to go after, and how it plans to win them, before any writing starts.

When does a business need it?

The usual signs: you bid on almost everything that comes in; win rates are low and the bid team is exhausted; proposals are copied from the last one; you find out who the competitors were after you lose.

What do you get at the end?

Typically: A bid or no-bid framework and governance; win strategy for live pursuits; proposal reviews against the scoring model; pricing and commercial sense-checks; lessons-learned reviews on wins and losses. See how pricing works.

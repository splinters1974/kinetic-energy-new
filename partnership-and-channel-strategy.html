Partnership and channel strategy

The right partners open doors faster and cheaper than you can on your own. The wrong ones take up management time and deliver nothing.

Start a conversation

Most partnerships fail for the same reason: nobody agreed who does what, who gets paid what, and what success looks like. We help you pick fewer partners and set them up properly.

Decide what a partner is for

We start with what you need. More coverage, specialist capability, a stronger proposition, credibility in a new market or funding. Different needs mean different partners, and not every gap needs a partnership to fill it.

Choose carefully

We assess the options, from resellers and developers to technology alliances, installers, funders and sector specialists. We test the commercial fit and the risk, and check that incentives line up so both sides actually want the deal to work.

Make the commercials simple

We help design partner models that are easy to run: margin, responsibilities, routes to revenue and governance. The goal is a partner network that adds reach without adding cost or confusion.

Common questions

Quick answers

What is a partnership and channel strategy?

A partnership and channel strategy sets out which partners a business sells with or through, such as resellers, installers, funders and technology partners, and how the money and responsibilities are shared.

When does a business need one?

The usual signs: you have lots of partners and little partner revenue; partner agreements are vague on who owns the customer; you need to scale faster than hiring allows; a partner relationship has stalled and nobody knows why.

What do you get at the end?

Typically: partner strategy and target partner profiles; assessment of current and potential partners; commercial model and margin structure; partner governance and review rhythm; joint go-to-market plan for priority partners. See how pricing works.
